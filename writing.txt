ROMAN FEVER
Written by
Abi Smith, Saara Uthmaan, and Lebron Castro

1 INT. HOTEL BAR. AFTERNOON.

MRS. SLADE and MRS. ANSLEY (mid 50s, visibly refined and wealthy. Ansley looks reserved and elegant, Slade is dressed more casual and has visibly aged Botox) are sitting across from each other at a table for two, chatting. Half-eaten appetizers remain neglected on their plates, while their wine glasses are almost empty. They're seated next to the window, but not quite paying attention to the view. Mrs. Ansley is busying herself by knitting a thick and frumpy sweater.

A waiter walks past the pair as Mrs. Slade finishes the last of her Aperol spritz cocktail.

MRS. SLADE
(waving glass in front of waiter)
Mm, and another one of these, dear.

MRS. ANSLEY
Goodness, the sun hasn't even set.

MRS. SLADE
Oh, it's just to have on hand! I like it better when the ice melts anyway.

MRS. ANSLEY
Sure, sure.

MRS. SLADE
Don't be like that. We're still young! Don't let our daughters get to you, now.

MRS. ANSLEY
I suppose, I suppose. Still, they aren't far off the mark, are they?

Both look down at the sweater she's knitting.

MRS. SLADE
(cackling)
That's your problem, dear. I thought you a more fashionable woman.

MRS. ANSLEY
I've found comfort to be more important these days.

MRS. SLADE
Bah. If that's so, take a drink. Come on — this is nothing! Compared to our daughters - off gallivanting with those boys in Umbria. They're probably wasted as we speak! Good for them.

MRS. ANSLEY
(soft chuckle)
We won't be seeing them at dinner, I reckon.

MRS. SLADE
Nonsense! If there's anything I've taught Jenny, it's to be punctual. A lady like her can't afford to be late. Not like your lovely Barbie.

Subtly, the perspective switches to Mrs. Slade's viewpoint. Mrs. Ansley looks calm and collected in her seat. The waiter comes over and drops off another cocktail, which Mrs. Slade sips at vigorously.

MRS. SLADE (CONT'D)
You know, Jenny is lovely. The perfect girl, I'd say. She mothers me more than I mother her. I just wish she was a bit more fun at times. Barbie has that spirit to her! That fire! That passion!

MRS. ANSLEY
I think you need to lay off the drinks, dear.

MRS. SLADE
(Slamming her glass down with fervor)
But it's true! Her and that Campolieri boy are such a stunning match - a model and a business heir - my goodness! You'd think romance wasn't dead. Poor Jenny doesn't stand a chance against her. How did you and Horace create someone so rowdy anyway?

The perspective switches to Mrs. Ansley. She reaches over to her own cocktail, downing the whole thing rather slowly. Mrs. Slade is leaning over the table, calling for more drinks. Mrs. Ansley does not respond for an uncomfortable amount of time, her knitting needles slowing to a halt.

MRS. ANSLEY
You overrate her.

MRS. SLADE
She's a showstopper.

MRS. ANSLEY
She's an angel, Alita.

MRS. SLADE
Yes, yes. But you know what I mean.

Mrs. Slade's eyes drift to a young couple a few tables over. They're talking loudly, clearly intimate and content with each other. Her hands bunch up on the tablecloth.

MRS. SLADE
So. Rome. Land of romance. It's good to meet back up here, it's refreshing. What a surprise to run into you!

MRS. ANSLEY
Oh - yes, wasn't it? It was delightful to see you here. I thought it was only me feeling a bit reminiscent.

MRS. SLADE
Now you're really talking like an old hag, Grace. But - yes, I should give you that, you're right. I miss Delphin oh so very much. I do love it here, but nostalgia has its ups and downs.

MRS. ANSLEY
Mm, yes.

MRS. SLADE
Yes?

MRS. ANSLEY
Yes - you, ah, you had an air about you before, I should say. We all miss Delphin.

MRS. SLADE
And Horace.

MRS. ANSLEY
Yes, yes, and Horace.

Mrs. Slade is clearly aggravated and almost opens her mouth to say something fiery, but the couple from before catches her eye again as they exit the bar. She visibly deflates, clicking her tongue while grabbing at another cocktail to down it.

Meanwhile, the perspective goes to Mrs. Ansley. She's tuning Mrs. Slade out, her eyes now also following the leaving couple out of the room.

MRS. SLADE
(muffled)
Horace was a good man too, he was. But Delphin - oh, how the people view me now that he's gone! Your gallery is the talk of the circles, but it's always poor her, poor widow when it comes to me. The price I pay for marrying such a notable man!

Mrs. Ansley orders another Aperol on her side, and knits at a reinvigorated pace. She stares out into the Roman Colosseum in the far distance.

MRS. ANSLEY
Yes, well...

MRS. SLADE
(slams fist on table)
I'm a life coach, don't you know that? It's a damn respectable line of work.

MRS. ANSLEY
Sure, Alita.

MRS. SLADE
And yet everyone looks down on me. Ha!... ha, I say!... Ah, to hell with it, whatever! Let's catch up proper, shall we? No more doom and gloom!

INT. ROOFTOP BAR. DUSK.

The two ladies talk uneventfully for hours. Clouds pass by as the sunset comes and goes. Glasses begin to pile up on the table, and there's barely any room left for more. The tables near them are a bit farther away than they were before. Two waiters play rock, paper, scissors in the back to decide who has to walk up to them, and the one who loses forces a depreciating smile before approaching them.

Mrs. Slade has slung her jacket over her chair, laughing outrageously at anything and everything. Mrs. Ansley looks down to find her knitting project rather garbled.

WAITER
(collecting glasses on a plate)
Perhaps I could interest you ladies in a bottle? We've had our finest regional wines come in recently. Montepulciano, Barbera, Orvieto...

MRS. SLADE
Ahaha... yes, sounds good, boy. We'll take... yes, all three!

Ansley waves both her hands nervously in a rejection motion, blocking the very drunk Slade from the first-person perspective.

MRS. ANSLEY
Oh, no, no, no, no... that's far too much. Have some decorum.

MRS. SLADE
Aw, live a little! Some for the kids, come on!

MRS. ANSLEY
(after a long silence)
...Yes, fine - oh, to hell with it!

MRS. SLADE
Ha ha! Atta girl, that's my girl! You still got it in you!

The waiter smiles and nods politely, and returns with two bottles of wine nestled in a large bucket of ice along with two wine glasses. It's placed on the center of the table, with Mrs. Slade opening one and drinking straight from it immediately. Mrs. Ansley holds on to the side of the bucket for a second before taking her bottle as well, but pouring it into a wine glass instead.

MRS. SLADE (CONT'D)
(exaggerated glee)
When in Rome, right? Just like the old times! You remember that trip? Oh, it must've been over twenty years ago by now!

MRS. ANSLEY
Yes, it - well, the city has changed from then, hasn't it?

MRS. SLADE
No, no, everything's the same. This city, the wine... you especially! Ha!

Mrs. Slade chugs a good portion of the wine. Mrs. Ansley stumbles as she sweeps aside her knitting materials to grab at her bottle as well. Their vision starts to haze at the edges around here.

MRS. ANSLEY
Certainly I've changed. We all grow up, Alita. Even if this place is filled with nice memories...

MRS. SLADE
Oh, yeah? Yeah? So why're you here then? Wanna explain?

Mrs. Ansley looks away, staring off the building's edge instead.

MRS. ANSLEY
I... told you. Just a bit of memory lane travel. And, really, it's only because Barbie wanted to come here anyway -

MRS. SLADE
Memories of what? Huh?

In peaking annoyance, Mrs. Ansley sharply looks back at Mrs. Slade. In her vision, Mrs. Slade suddenly has some tears falling down her eyes, mascara flowing down her face. Mrs. Ansley is taken aback, and rubs her eyes in confusion.

MRS. ANSLEY
I - um, what...?

MRS. SLADE
You were sick, don't you remember? Si - i - i - ick! Right after the first day! What memories were pleasant here, huh!?

MRS. ANSLEY
You - you need to calm down. Goodness.

The perspective changes to Mrs. Slade again. Her voice is not as hysterical as Mrs. Ansley has been hallucinating it to be, but she is still clearly irritated. In her view, Mrs. Ansley is somewhat sparkling, with a beauty filter over her face. She has a permanent stone-faced, stoic expression.

MRS. SLADE
Sorry, sorry. Whatever. It's just - well, you know. With how your mother is, we were all shocked how you could catch anything. Specially something so nasty. It's like that roman fever our parents used to talk of. Remember, your awful great-aunt?

MRS. ANSLEY
Aunt Karen? Goodness, yes. Sending her little sister to pick flowers in the dead of winter, poor girl.

MRS. SLADE
But really, the reason was because they were after the same man, right?

MRS. ANSLEY
It's.. hm. Everyone has some messed up family drama, Alita. And the poor girl died from her illness. You shouldn't bring up the dead.

MRS. SLADE
(Pointing at Ansley's eyeline)
It's just...! You scared me that winter! You reminded me of that bitc... I mean, woman.

MRS. ANSLEY
Mm. Really? That's a surprise. Why?

MRS. SLADE
Well - at the time, I just got engaged to Delphin, and - ugh, lord, I don't know! Engagement is such a happy, anxious time, don't you think?

MRS. ANSLEY
Ah, well... I wouldn't know.

MRS. SLADE
There's no Roman fever now, but the Colosseum is cold at night. Specially after a scorching day in the Italian sun. You'd know about that, wouldn't you, Grace? Isn't that why you caught a cold that night?

MRS. ANSLEY
The Colosseum?

MRS. SLADE
The gates lock at night, but pesky little shits always find a way to get in. You know how it is, young love. You know that, right? Everyone knows about it.

MRS. ANSLEY
Hmph. No, I don't recall.

MRS. SLADE
(Leaning forward)
Oh, come on! You know, right? You don't remember? How you ever caught that cold?

MRS. ANSLEY
(rolling her eyes)
It was a long time ago, Alita.

MRS. SLADE
(mocking pitch and tone)
Mm, yeah, okay. Long time ago. Fine. Doesn't matter anyways. You recovered, after all.

Mrs. Slade's phone buzzes on the table. She glances down quickly. A text from Jenny pops up. The text reads "Heading back now mom! Making a rez. Where do you wanna eat?"

The perspective changes to Mrs. Ansley again. Mrs. Slade's hair is now fried and stringy, she's sobbing angrily and being extremely emotional. She shoves her brick phone into the ice bucket in front of her, rummaging it around in great offense.

MRS. SLADE (CONT'D)
Ugh! What even led you to the Colosseum that night?

MRS. ANSLEY
What made you think I was there?

MRS. SLADE
(mocking)
What made you think I was there? I can repeat every word of the letter that sent you running there. You're fucking pathetic.

MRS. ANSLEY
What? I - no, don't...

MRS. SLADE
(pouting exaggeratedly, standing up and leaning in)
Awww, why not? Oh, you thought it was so romantic, didn't you? Little love letters by hand like from the "old days"? Here, I'll remind you. "My darling love, my heart aches with desire, I can't live a second longer without meeting you! I just have to see you alone, so come to the Colosseum at midnight tomorrow. The side gate will be unlocked. I pinky promise my stupid fiance won't find out!"

Mrs. Slade sweeps off her wine glass from the table, shattering it against the floor. The perspective moves out from both of them to the whole bar. Everyone is staring at them and giving them weirded-out looks. She awkwardly and slowly sits back down. Notably, both women are back to their normal states, although visibly taken aback and reeling, tension filling the air between them.

MRS. SLADE (CONT'D)
(quietly hissing)
Bitch.

MRS. ANSLEY
(disinterested)
I told you to calm down. And, yes, I remember it. By heart. How do you know of it? I burned that letter immediately.

MRS. SLADE
Of course. Had to cover up your paper trail right? You want to know how I know it?

Silence ensues for a few moments.

MRS. SLADE
I know the letter, because I fucking wrote it.

Ansley's perspective. Mrs. Slade is now ripping at the tablecloth and throwing things around. Because this is a hallucination, no one is actually stopping her.

MRS. SLADE
(pointing a fork at Mrs. Ansley)
I wrote the fucking letter, dumbass!

The wine glass Mrs. Ansley is holding slips from her grip.

MRS. ANSLEY
You - you wrote it?

MRS. SLADE
(pointing to her brain)
Did I stutter? What's not clicking for you?

Mrs. Ansley begins to gently up, making the edges of her vision watery.

MRS. ANSLEY
It... it was the only letter I ever got from Delphin.

MRS. SLADE
(Gleefully smug)
And I wrote it by hand! I was the one he was engaged to, you remember that?

MRS. ANSLEY
I do. I'm not trying to make excuses.

MRS. SLADE
Liar, liar, pants on fire.

Slade's perspective. Ansley's expression is still, unchanging, and utterly uncaring.

MRS. ANSLEY
I went. What about it?

MRS. SLADE
I actually hated you, you know? I saw you in love with him and I hated you... your quiet, "innocent", sweet persona, and the way he looked at you... God. Nothing slipped past me. I wanted you gone. At least for a few weeks so he'd forget about you, and whatever you did to hoe yourself out for him.

MRS. SLADE (CONT'D)
(Drawing a breath, and composing herself)
So I wrote that. Sent your ass into the cold. Didn't think you'd actually get sick.

MRS. ANSLEY
All these years and you still hate me. It's a bit sad for you.

MRS. SLADE
Piss off. It's only sometimes. But I think I just needed the weight off my shoulders. After all these years, I thought it wouldn't matter. And that you would even laugh it off.

MRS. ANSLEY
Laugh? Hm...

MRS. SLADE
You tried to take him from me. Ha! Nice try! You failed. I kept him. For twenty-five years at least.

MRS. ANSLEY
Yes. Yes, you did.

MRS. SLADE
The only thing you ever had of his was a letter, one that he never wrote.

Ansley's perspective. Mrs. Slade looks distraught and completely crushed, and her weeping and sniffling is a constant background noise.

MRS. ANSLEY
But it wasn't. That night was worth it, and I didn't have to wait.

MRS. SLADE
What?

MRS. ANSLEY
He was already waiting for me when I showed up.

MRS. SLADE
(devastated, through gritted teeth)
That's - that's not possible. How'd he could he know?

MRS. ANSLEY
I answered his letter.

MRS. SLADE
(Stuttering)
You... y-you answered...? You crazy bitch, how could you -

MRS. ANSLEY
I responded and told him I'd be there, without a doubt.

MRS. SLADE
Fuck you! How could you fucking do that to me, you skank!? You're just a follower, you're submissive - doing whatever I do, including my fiancée, apparently - what the hell's your problem? Fuck!

From here, the perspectives rapidly change between them with each dialogue. With each switch, Mrs. Slade progressively wails louder and looks like an increasingly hot mess, while Mrs. Ansley looks increasingly untouchable, more and more exaggerated beauty filters with a cold expression and glowing until her face is barely visible.

MRS. ANSLEY
What part of calm down do you not understand?

MRS. SLADE
I just - hic - I just don't fucking get it, do you hate me or something - why did he even fucking go to you anyway -

MRS. ANSLEY
Well, that's not quite my problem at all.

MRS. SLADE
You - you need to fuck off! I hate you, I always hated you - I don't know why I ever kept your bitch ass around - all you are is a whore, you - you!

MRS. ANSLEY
I may have seemed easy, but I was just smart. Smarter than you apparently.

MRS. SLADE
Go to hell, Grace. I bet Delphin only fucked you because he felt bad for your sad fucking ass -

MRS. ANSLEY
And yet still I win.

The hysterical Mrs. Slade lets out a shrill scream, only to be cut off by silence. The perspective shifts back out to normal view again. The fun part of their drunkenness is over. Both women are back to their normal states, although visibly taken aback and reeling, tension filling the air between them.

MRS. ANSLEY
I feel sorry for you Alita.

MRS. SLADE
Why would you ever feel sorry for me?

MRS. ANSLEY
Because I didn't have to wait that night. Like you had hoped.

MRS. SLADE
At the end of the day, he was mine. As much as it hurts me that you betrayed me, I still built a life with him.

MRS. ANSLEY
Betrayed? Well, that's a strong word for someone who consistently has tried to be superior to me. You're insecure. Of my looks, my life, even my daughter... You have to grow up someday. Maybe it'll happen on the next trip.

MRS. SLADE
Will there be a next trip at this rate?

MRS. ANSLEY
Perhaps not.

The women wave over the waiter to pay their bill. They're fuming, but cannot find the words to express themselves. After receiving another text from her daughter, Mrs. Slade finally speaks as they rise from their table and begin to walk down the stairs towards the exit.

MRS. SLADE
At least I have my Jenny. What can you say you have from Delphin?

MRS. ANSLEY
(Smiling innocently, turning around to make eye contact with Slade)
Me? I have Barbie.

EXT. ROOFTOP BAR. NIGHT.
